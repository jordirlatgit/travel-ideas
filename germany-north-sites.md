# North Germany — Family Travel Guide
### Budget-friendly · Public transport · Wildlife · 3–4 days

A practical guide for 2 adults + 2 kids exploring northern Germany without a car.
The region is ideal for low-cost family travel: vast national parks, free beaches,
reliable regional trains, and extraordinary wildlife that costs nothing to see.

---

## The Single Best Money-Saving Move

Buy a **Schleswig-Holstein Ticket** (€25/group, weekdays) or a **Lower Saxony Ticket** (€25/group, weekdays).
One ticket covers the whole family on all regional trains and buses within the state —
including Hamburg — for an entire day. Children under 6 travel free on all Deutsche Bahn
regional services. Children 6–14 travel free when accompanied by a parent on a Länder ticket.

## 20 Places to Visit

---

### 1. Lübeck
**Region:** Schleswig-Holstein | **Cost level:** Low

UNESCO-listed Hanseatic city with fairy-tale brick Gothic towers. The old town is fully
explorable on foot — don't miss the Holstentor gate and the old merchant quarters.

- **Getting there:** Direct trains from Hamburg (45 min). Well-connected hub.
- **For the kids:** The Holstentor and climbing St. Peter's Church tower for panoramic views.
- **What's free:** Old town strolling, Holstentor exterior, Dom courtyard.
- **Budget tip:** Day trip from Hamburg. Marzipan tasting at Niederegger is affordable.
- **Local tip:** Get the Schleswig-Holstein Ticket (€25/group) for unlimited regional travel on weekdays.

---

### 2. Travemünde
**Region:** Schleswig-Holstein | **Cost level:** Free beach

Lübeck's seaside resort at the mouth of the Trave river. Sandy beaches, lighthouse,
and ferry connections to Scandinavia — a lovely half-day addition to Lübeck.

- **Getting there:** S-Bahn from Lübeck (30 min).
- **For the kids:** Wide sandy beach, rock-skipping in the Trave estuary, watching giant ferries depart.
- **What's free:** Beach access. Lighthouse exterior.
- **Budget tip:** Pack your own picnic. Beach chairs cost extra — bring a blanket.
- **Local tip:** Combine with Lübeck on the same day ticket.
- 🦅 **Wildlife:** Grey seals often spotted in the Baltic, especially in autumn and winter.

---

### 3. Hamburg
**Region:** Hamburg | **Cost level:** Low

Germany's second city. The Speicherstadt warehouse district, Alster lakes, and Stadtpark
are free highlights. The Miniatur Wunderland is pricey but unforgettable for kids.

- **Getting there:** Central hub — ICE, IC and S-Bahn connections everywhere.
- **For the kids:** Miniatur Wunderland (book ahead), Stadtpark paddling, Alsterdampfer boat rides.
- **What's free:** Speicherstadt streets, Alster lakeside, Stadtpark, Hafenpromenade walk.
- **Budget tip:** Hamburg Card gives free public transport + museum discounts. Use HVV app.
- **Local tip:** The HafenCity waterfront walk is free and very impressive for all ages.
- 🦅 **Wildlife:** Cormorants and herons nest along the Alster lakes in the city.

---

### 4. Nationalpark Hamburgisches Wattenmeer
**Region:** Hamburg / Schleswig-Holstein | **Cost level:** Free entry

UNESCO World Heritage mudflats south of Hamburg. Guided wadden walks (Wattwandern)
reveal an alien world of mud, worms, shrimp, and birds at low tide.

- **Getting there:** S-Bahn to Neugraben, then bus to Stade. Or direct bus to Cuxhaven.
- **For the kids:** Squishing through the mud barefoot is an unforgettable experience.
- **What's free:** The park. Guided walks (~€5/person) are highly recommended.
- **Budget tip:** Bring old clothes and waterproof sandals — you WILL get muddy.
- **Local tip:** Check the tide tables before you go — walks are only possible at low tide.
- 🦅 **Wildlife:** Millions of migrating birds (knots, dunlins). Seals haul out on sandbanks. Shrimp and crabs visible in the mud.

---

### 5. Cuxhaven
**Region:** Lower Saxony | **Cost level:** Free beach

Where the Elbe meets the North Sea. The Duhnen and Döse beaches are broad and clean;
the Kugelbake landmark marks the border between the North Sea and the Elbe.

- **Getting there:** Train from Hamburg Hauptbahnhof (2 hrs).
- **For the kids:** North Sea mud baths, giant crab observations at low tide, lighthouse visits.
- **What's free:** Beach. Kugelbake walk.
- **Budget tip:** Camping and holiday apartments are cheaper than Sylt. Great value.
- **Local tip:** The Schlickrutschen (mud slide) at Duhnen is hilarious for kids.
- 🦅 **Wildlife:** Harbour porpoises occasionally seen from the shore. Common seals at the Elbe sandbanks.

---

### 6. Nationalpark Schleswig-Holsteinisches Wattenmeer
**Region:** Schleswig-Holstein | **Cost level:** Free entry

The largest national park in Central Europe. This tidal world stretching from the Danish
border to Cuxhaven is a UNESCO World Heritage Site — take a seal-watching boat tour from Husum.

- **Getting there:** Train to Husum (from Hamburg, 1h 45min), then ferry or bus to wadden edge.
- **For the kids:** Seal-watching boat trips, sand castle building on pristine beaches.
- **What's free:** The park. Seal boat tours ~€15/adult, €8/child.
- **Budget tip:** Husum is a charming, affordable base with cheap guesthouses.
- **Local tip:** August–October is the best season to see seals hauled out on the sandbanks.
- 🦅 **Wildlife:** Grey and harbour seals on sandbanks. Huge flocks of migrating birds. Spoonbills nesting on islands.

---

### 7. Husum
**Region:** Schleswig-Holstein | **Cost level:** Low

The 'grey city on the grey sea' — a charming market town with a colourful harbour,
Theodor Storm Museum, and a famous spring tulip bloom in the castle garden.

- **Getting there:** Train from Hamburg (1h 45min). Good base for the National Park.
- **For the kids:** Tiny harbour boats, castle park walk, hot chocolate in the harbour cafés.
- **What's free:** Harbour walk, castle park, historic town centre.
- **Budget tip:** Try the Nordsee Krabbenbrötchen (shrimp sandwich) — a cheap, authentic local meal.
- **Local tip:** Stay here as a base to avoid expensive island ferry costs.
- 🦅 **Wildlife:** Grey seals frequently visible from the harbour quay, especially at dusk.

---

### 8. Sylt (day trip)
**Region:** Schleswig-Holstein | **Cost level:** Train access

Germany's most famous North Sea island. Huge sand dunes, dramatic cliffs at Kampen,
and the List point at Germany's northernmost tip. The island is wealthy but the landscape is free.

- **Getting there:** Train direct from Hamburg (2h 30min) — no car needed! Bikes rentable on-island.
- **For the kids:** Dune climbing, sandcastle competitions, seal station at List.
- **What's free:** All beaches. Dune landscape. List peninsula.
- **Budget tip:** Avoid restaurants — pack your own food, as Sylt is very expensive to eat in.
- **Local tip:** Ride the island by bike — rental is ~€12/day. Rent at the Westerland station.
- 🦅 **Wildlife:** Common eiders and oystercatchers nesting in the dunes. Seals at List.

---

### 9. Flensburg
**Region:** Schleswig-Holstein | **Cost level:** Free highlights

Germany's northernmost city on a fjord, with colourful merchants' courtyards, the famous
rum tradition, and a beautiful waterfront. Very underrated by tourists.

- **Getting there:** Train from Hamburg (2h 20min). Or from Husum (45 min).
- **For the kids:** Flensburg Fjord swimming, the old merchant courtyards are like a maze, free museum in the Nordertor.
- **What's free:** Fjord shoreline walk, courtyard exploration, Marienkirche.
- **Budget tip:** Very affordable city. Great local bakeries. Pack bread and cheese for a fjord picnic.
- **Local tip:** The Museumsberg has excellent panoramic views and is free on the first Sunday of the month.
- 🦅 **Wildlife:** Fallow deer in the Freienwill forests just outside town. Seabirds on the fjord.

---

### 10. Ratzeburg
**Region:** Schleswig-Holstein | **Cost level:** Free nature

A cathedral island town surrounded by four lakes — one of northern Germany's most scenic
small cities. The 24km lake circuit by bike or on foot is spectacular.

- **Getting there:** Train from Hamburg (1h 15min via Büchen).
- **For the kids:** Paddle boats on the lake, eagle spotting with binoculars, flat lakeshore paths for small legs.
- **What's free:** Lake circuit walk/cycle, cathedral, all lake shores.
- **Budget tip:** Day trip from Hamburg or Lübeck. Very cheap local restaurants.
- **Local tip:** Bring binoculars — white-tailed eagle sightings are nearly guaranteed in the morning.
- 🦅 **Wildlife:** White-tailed eagles (Seeadler) regularly spotted over the lakes. Ospreys in summer. Otters in the reed beds.

---

### 11. Mölln
**Region:** Schleswig-Holstein | **Cost level:** Free lakes

The Till Eulenspiegel town, surrounded by the Herzogtum Lauenburg lake district.
Over 200 lakes within cycling distance — clear, clean, and free to swim in.

- **Getting there:** Train from Hamburg (1h via Büchen).
- **For the kids:** Wild swimming in crystal-clear lakes, the Eulenspiegel fountain, flat cycling trails.
- **What's free:** All lake swimming. Forests.
- **Budget tip:** Pack swimsuits and a towel — wild swimming is the main activity here.
- **Local tip:** The Dreifelder Moor trail passes through old forests — excellent for spotting red kites.
- 🦅 **Wildlife:** Red kites soaring over the forests. Beavers in the Sterley area. Kingfishers on the river.

---

### 12. Kiel
**Region:** Schleswig-Holstein | **Cost level:** Free waterfront

The state capital on the Kieler Förde. The Ostseeküstenradweg (Baltic cycling route)
starts here. The GEOMAR aquarium is excellent and cheap for marine wildlife.

- **Getting there:** Train from Hamburg (1h 20min). Or bus from Lübeck.
- **For the kids:** Free open-air ship museum at the Schifffahrtsmuseum, seal feeding at GEOMAR, Hörn bridge watching.
- **What's free:** Holtenau locks (watch ships pass), Düsternbrook shore walk, Stadtwald forest.
- **Budget tip:** Kieler Förde ferry across the water is only €2 and gives a great maritime view.
- **Local tip:** The Schifffahrtsmuseum (maritime museum) has free entry and is great for kids who love ships.
- 🦅 **Wildlife:** The GEOMAR aquarium has Baltic Sea fish, spider crabs, and seals in outdoor pools.

---

### 13. Eckernförde
**Region:** Schleswig-Holstein | **Cost level:** Free beach

A hidden gem — a charming Baltic town with a stunning free beach and a shallow, warm
lagoon (Binnensee) that's perfect for young children.

- **Getting there:** Train from Kiel (30 min). Or from Hamburg (1h 50min).
- **For the kids:** The Binnensee (inner lagoon) is shallow and child-safe. Sand beach. Seal watching.
- **What's free:** All beaches. Lagoon. Cliff walk.
- **Budget tip:** Very affordable, much less touristy than Sylt or Timmendorf.
- **Local tip:** The fish smokehouse on the harbour sells smoked sprats for €2 — a great cheap lunch.
- 🦅 **Wildlife:** Common seals haul out on the sandbanks at the fjord mouth — often visible from the shore.

---

### 14. Fehmarn Island
**Region:** Schleswig-Holstein | **Cost level:** Free beaches

Connected to the mainland by bridge — you can reach it by train! Long beaches,
dramatic chalk cliffs at the south, and the best Baltic sunshine in Germany.

- **Getting there:** Train from Hamburg via Lübeck or Kiel (~2h). Bridge makes it train-accessible.
- **For the kids:** Wallnau wildlife reserve with observation towers, beach Olympics, seal watching in the Fehmarn Belt.
- **What's free:** Beaches. Wallnau reserve walking trails. Lighthouses viewable from outside.
- **Budget tip:** Campsite is the best budget option. Very family-friendly.
- **Local tip:** Visit the Wallnau reserve early morning for the best bird sightings.
- 🦅 **Wildlife:** Avocets and terns nesting at Wallnau nature reserve. Harbour porpoises in the Fehmarn Belt.

---

### 15. Lüneburg
**Region:** Lower Saxony | **Cost level:** Low

A medieval salt-trade city with crooked half-timbered houses — the buildings sank into
the salt mines beneath. The historic old town is free to wander, like stepping into a fairy tale.

- **Getting there:** Train from Hamburg (35 min). Easy day trip.
- **For the kids:** The tilting houses look like something from a Dr. Seuss book. The salt museum is interesting and cheap.
- **What's free:** Entire old town, Am Sande square, the Ilmenau riverside.
- **Budget tip:** Excellent base for Lüneburger Heide. Cheaper accommodation than Hamburg.
- **Local tip:** Combine with a Heide visit in the same day using a group train ticket.
- 🦅 **Wildlife:** Storks nest on rooftops in surrounding villages. Bats at dusk over the old town.

---

### 16. Lüneburger Heide
**Region:** Lower Saxony | **Cost level:** Free nature

A vast heathland that turns bright purple in late August when the heather blooms.
One of Germany's most iconic landscapes — endless open space, wild ponies, and ancient burial mounds.

- **Getting there:** Train to Lüneburg or Soltau (from Hamburg), then regional bus to Wilsede.
- **For the kids:** See the wild Heidschnucken sheep, horse-drawn carriage rides through the heath (cheap), picnic in the purple heather.
- **What's free:** All trails. Totengrund valley.
- **Budget tip:** Visit in late August for the famous purple bloom — the heathland's peak season.
- **Local tip:** The Totengrund hollow near Wilsede is the most photogenic spot. No cars allowed — walk or take the horse carriage.
- 🦅 **Wildlife (exceptional):** Wild Heidschnucken sheep, buzzards and red kites overhead, European hares, wild boar at dusk. Rare adders in the heath.

---

### 17. Stade
**Region:** Lower Saxony | **Cost level:** Free highlights

A perfectly preserved Hanseatic town near Hamburg with a beautiful old harbour,
half-timbered warehouses, and a charming historic centre that rivals Lübeck with far fewer crowds.

- **Getting there:** Train or S-Bahn from Hamburg (45 min). Or bus from Cuxhaven.
- **For the kids:** The old cranes and barges at the harbour, small local museum, the Elbe meadow walk.
- **What's free:** Entire old town, harbour walk, Elbe meadows.
- **Budget tip:** Very few tourists means excellent value restaurants and guesthouses.
- **Local tip:** Visit on a Saturday for the market on Fischmarkt square.
- 🦅 **Wildlife:** White storks nesting on rooftops in spring/summer. Oystercatchers on the Elbe shore.

---

### 18. Celle
**Region:** Lower Saxony | **Cost level:** Free old town

One of Germany's best-preserved half-timbered cities, with over 500 buildings from
five centuries. The palace garden is free and beautiful.

- **Getting there:** Train from Hamburg (1h 15min). Or from Lüneburg (45 min).
- **For the kids:** Palace garden with peacocks, the leaning old town streets, free courtyard exploration.
- **What's free:** Old town, palace park, French Garden, all church courtyards.
- **Budget tip:** Excellent budget base for exploring the Lüneburger Heide and Aller river.
- **Local tip:** The Tierpark Celle has native German wildlife (deer, foxes, wild boar) for a small entry fee.
- 🦅 **Wildlife:** White storks on rooftops (spring-summer). Red kites in the Südheide forests nearby.

---

### 19. Schleswig
**Region:** Schleswig-Holstein | **Cost level:** Low

An ancient Viking city on the Schlei fjord. The Viking Museum Haithabu is world-class,
and Gottorf Castle has extensive grounds. The Schlei estuary is gorgeous.

- **Getting there:** Train from Hamburg (2h) or from Husum (30 min).
- **For the kids:** Viking Museum Haithabu (replicas of longships, reconstructed Viking village), Schlei boat trips.
- **What's free:** Schlei shoreline walk, Gottorf park, cathedral.
- **Budget tip:** The Viking museum combo ticket (~€12/adult, kids cheaper) is excellent value.
- **Local tip:** Haithabu is one of the most important Viking sites in the world — buy the combined castle + Viking museum ticket.
- 🦅 **Wildlife:** Whooper swans and greylag geese on the Schlei in winter. Marsh harriers in the reeds.

---

### 20. Nationalpark Jasmund (Rügen)
**Region:** Mecklenburg-Vorpommern | **Cost level:** Free park

Germany's smallest national park with the most dramatic scenery — the white chalk cliffs
of Königsstuhl rising 118m above the Baltic Sea. Ancient beech forests behind them are UNESCO World Heritage.

- **Getting there:** Train from Hamburg to Stralsund (2h 30min), then to Sassnitz on Rügen. Bus to Hagen/Königsstuhl.
- **For the kids:** The chalk cliff walk is dramatic and memorable. The forest UNESCO trail is manageable for kids.
- **What's free:** All trails. Cliff viewing. Forests. Small visitor centre fee (~€7).
- **Budget tip:** Camp at Sassnitz — cheapest way to enjoy the park. Rügen is affordable off-peak.
- **Local tip:** The best view of the Königsstuhl cliffs is from the sea — take the boat tour from Sassnitz harbour.
- 🦅 **Wildlife:** White-tailed eagles nesting in the beech forest. Fallow deer. Peregrine falcons on the chalk cliffs.

## Suggested 3–4 Day Itinerary

Base yourself in **Hamburg** throughout. Its central location and superb transport links
make it the ideal hub — you save on accommodation by avoiding pricier coastal resorts,
and everything below is reachable by regional train.

### Day 1 — Hamburg base camp
1. Arrive Hamburg. Walk the **Speicherstadt** warehouse district (free). Lunch at the Fischmarkt.
2. Afternoon: S-Bahn to **Lüneburg** (35 min) — wander the tilting medieval houses. Return for dinner.
3. Evening: stroll the Alster lake shore — watch cormorants and herons.

### Day 2 — Wadden Sea & seals
1. Early train to **Cuxhaven** (2h). Mud-walking at the Wattenmeer National Park — check tide tables first!
2. Lunch on the beach. Look for harbour porpoises from the Kugelbake viewpoint.
3. Train to **Stade** (1h 15min) — walk the perfectly preserved Hanseatic harbour. Return to Hamburg.

### Day 3 — Lübeck & Baltic coast
1. Train to **Lübeck** (45 min). Walk the UNESCO old town: Holstentor, Buddenbrookhaus, St Mary's Church.
2. S-Bahn to **Travemünde** (30 min). Baltic beach afternoon — kids on the sand, adults watching for grey seals.
3. Train to **Ratzeburg** (15 min). Evening lake walk — prime time for white-tailed eagle spotting.

### Day 4 — Heathland & wildlife (optional extension)
1. Train to **Lüneburg** (35 min from Hamburg), then bus to **Lüneburger Heide**. Purple heather blooms in late August.
2. See wild Heidschnucken sheep and buzzards overhead in the open heathland.
3. Return via Lüneburg. Stop at **Celle** (45 min) for a quick old town wander before the journey home.

## Budget Summary & Key Tips

**Transport:**
- Schleswig-Holstein Ticket: €25/group (weekdays) — covers the whole family, all day, all regional trains + buses in SH and Hamburg
- Lower Saxony Ticket: €25/group (weekdays) — same concept for Lüneburg, Cuxhaven, Celle
- Children under 6: free on all Deutsche Bahn regional services
- Children 6–14: free when accompanied by a parent on a Länder ticket

**Food:**
- Buy picnics from REWE, Edeka, or Aldi — supermarkets are everywhere and prices are low
- Krabbenbrötchen (North Sea shrimp sandwich) €3–5 — the region's iconic cheap street food, found at every harbour
- Fish smokehouses in Eckernförde and similar ports sell smoked sprats and herring for €2

**Accommodation:**
- Stay in Hamburg as your base — central and cheaper than coastal resorts
- Campsites on Rügen and Fehmarn are the most affordable way to do longer stays

**Wildlife is free:**
- Seals, eagles, migratory birds, wild ponies — the best experiences cost nothing
- Bring binoculars and a field guide to native birds
- Check tide tables if visiting the Wattenmeer (walks only possible at low tide)
